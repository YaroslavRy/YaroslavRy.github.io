Neuron Sim

Specimen 04 WebGL · gl.POINTS Leaky integrate-and-fire · sparse connectivity

Every point is a neuron: it leaks charge toward rest, occasionally fires on its own, and — once it crosses threshold — kicks a set of other neurons. In realistic mode those connections are distance-based: nearby neurons are more likely to be linked, connection strength decays with distance, and the signal takes longer to arrive the farther it travels — same idea as the original simulation, just accelerated with a spatial grid so it still runs at thousands of neurons instead of dozens. Random mode is a control condition: same neuron count, but every connection target is picked uniformly at random anywhere in the population, delivered instantly. Switch between them below and watch the raster and phase portrait change shape.

The phase portrait plots population spiking activity against its rate of change, one dot per frame, with old dots fading — this is what makes it possible to see the dynamics rather than just the raw spikes. Distance-based connectivity tends to produce tighter, more structured loops (local clusters syncing up); fully random connectivity tends to look noisier and less organized, since there's no spatial structure for activity to travel coherently through.
